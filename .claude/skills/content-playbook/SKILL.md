# Content & Authority Playbook

Source: adapted from an X growth-marketing article covering X, YouTube, and short-form (Reels/TikTok) organic growth. The content craft and platform-algorithm mechanics are sound and reused below. One section of the source — fake-identity/geo-spoofed account farming for Instagram/TikTok — is **deliberately dropped, not reused in any form**. See "What's excluded" for why.

## Fit check — read this before doing anything else

- **X and YouTube tactics below are Track A only** (see `growth-playbook` for the track split) — they assume an English-reachable audience actively using these platforms as a discovery surface: `jumelleCafe`, `BeanPath`/`beanspath`, `co3data`, `coopmanager`, `AgriSight`, `CongoCSPC`.
- **Short-form (Reels/TikTok) craft applies to both tracks** — Track B products (`kivukazi`, `kazi`, `cafekivucongo`, `aryv logistics`, `boutique`, etc.) have real Congolese audiences on these platforms; the hook/retention/reward structure and posting cadence below transfer, the US-audience-targeting setup does not.
- Single-client/internal tools (`comptable`, `heritage-app`, `Patrimoine`, `fikiri_id`) — this doesn't apply, say so rather than forcing it.
- This is about **building an ongoing audience/authority**, not a single event. For launch-day X mechanics specifically, use `launch-video`. For the broader 7-tactic acquisition checklist, use `growth-playbook`.

## X (Twitter): what resonates

- **Niche = audience.** The account's niche must be 1:1 with who buys the product — a Track A founder posting about AI/dev tooling in general won't convert into `coopmanager` or `AgriSight` customers as reliably as posting specifically in the cooperative-management/agtech niche. Pick the niche the actual buyers are in, not the one that's easiest to write about.
- **Simple, non-try-hard profile.** Real name or plain handle, a one-line bio stating what you do (not a sales pitch), no role-descriptor-in-name ("Founder of X — B2B SaaS for Y"). Authenticity reads better than an obviously sales-optimized profile.
- **Value-first content**, written plainly (short sentences, no jargon-gating), diversified across formats (threads, quick insights, contrarian takes) so engagement doesn't plateau on one format.
- **The "outside push" for a cold-start account**: pair genuinely valuable content with a deliberate early push from a bigger, real-engagement account in the same niche (e.g., an affiliate arrangement) rather than posting into the void. This is the same mechanic as `growth-playbook` tactic #4 (paid UGC/creator push) applied to a founder's own account — **the same caveats apply**: get the user's sign-off before any payment, and if the arrangement is paid or product-for-post, disclosure is required, flag it rather than letting it pass silently.
- **Avoid what actively hurts an account**: reply-guy behavior, engagement pods / follow-for-follow (these dilute the platform's relationship-graph signal and can suppress reach, not boost it — don't recommend them even informally).
- Algorithm context worth knowing when advising: engagement velocity (first ~30 min), dwell time, and the relationship-graph (a post is tested on the author's immediate network first, then expands) all matter more than raw follower count. Use this to set expectations, not as something to game.

## YouTube: what resonates

- **Channel authority compounds** — a new channel is throttled relative to an established one with the same content. Don't promise fast wins; set expectations around consistency over months, not days.
- **Search vs. browse/recommended are different funnels.** Search traffic (especially high-intent keyword patterns like "best [solution]", "[solution] for [use case]", "how to [outcome]") converts far better than browse/recommended traffic because the viewer is already solution-aware. For Track A products with a real sales cycle (`coopmanager`, `AgriSight`, `CongoCSPC`), prioritize high-intent search topics over broad/viral ones.
- **Keyword research**: a tool like vidiq (unverified by me — suggest the category, let the user pick their own if they have a preference) can surface search volume and intent; the actual ranking mechanic is simpler than most SEO advice claims — put the keyword in the title and description once, and make sure the video content genuinely matches the search intent. Don't over-invest in tags/metadata tricks.
- **Every video should double as education + pitch** — teach a real sub-problem of what the product solves, prove competence by not gatekeeping the value, and position the product as the natural next step, rather than bolting a CTA onto an otherwise-generic explainer. This is a legitimate content-marketing technique (educate-and-convert), not deceptive — the viewer gets real value whether or not they buy.
- **Packaging (title + thumbnail) determines whether retention even gets a chance to matter** — spend real effort here before production, not after.

## Short-form (Reels/TikTok): what resonates

- **The 4-pillar structure**: hook in the first 2–3 seconds (curiosity gap, relatable pain, results-based, pattern interrupt), fast retention (cuts every 2–3s, get to the point immediately), a reward delivered within 5–15 seconds, and engineered engagement (a genuine question, a save-worthy checklist/reference, "share with someone who needs this").
- **Platform-specific top signal**: saves matter most on Instagram, shares and rewatch rate matter most on TikTok — shape the CTA accordingly.
- **Posting cadence discipline**: start with a low, consistent cadence (don't flood a cold account), scale up once something breaks through. TikTok tests each video independently regardless of follower count — a flop on video #3 doesn't predict video #15.
- **This is the same lever as `growth-playbook` tactics #4 and #5** (UGC creators, video > text) — use this section as the craft detail behind those tactics, on both tracks.

## What's excluded — and why

The source article's "account setup" instructions for Instagram/TikTok are **not reused in any form**: buying a residential proxy to fake a US IP, wiping a phone to impersonate "a brand-new American device," creating a US Apple ID with a fabricated US address, using a burner-number service to pass SMS verification, then running a scripted multi-day "warming" routine specifically designed to pass the platform's bot/spam detection (with a "follow test" gate, and discard-and-restart if detected).

This is identity-spoofing built to defeat a platform's own verification and anti-fraud systems — not a marketing technique, a detection-evasion one. It's excluded regardless of framing, the same way `launch-video` already declined to reuse coordinated-engagement mechanics from its own source thread.

It also fails on its own terms here: Track B products sell to Congolese customers in Goma/Kivu. Optimizing an account to look like a genuine US user targets the wrong audience for those products' actual buyers — it wouldn't achieve the stated goal even ignoring the ethics.

If a project genuinely needs a US-facing account (a Track A founder personally based outside DRC, for instance), that's just... using a real account from wherever the person actually is. No proxy, no fabricated identity needed.

## What to hand back when asked for a content/authority plan

- Platform + track fit (don't default to X/YouTube for a Track B product with no real audience there)
- The niche and the specific angle (what the founder/brand knows that's differentiated — not a copy of an existing account in the niche)
- A concrete cadence (X: daily value-first posts; YouTube: consistency over virality; short-form: low-and-steady until something breaks through)
- For any paid push (affiliate, UGC creator, shoutout) — explicit sign-off before outreach/payment, and a disclosure flag if it's paid or incentivized
- Never fabricate example accounts, viral posts, or tools as "verified" — say when something needs the user to confirm or research it live
